About the Villa Heights area

Villa Heights is an in-town neighborhood northeast of Uptown, between Plaza Midwood and NoDa. It developed in the early 20th century, and in recent decades it has seen substantial renovation and new construction.

About Villa Heights

Villa Heights began around 1900 as a subdivision marketed by real estate developer Clayton O. Brown. It is described as one of Charlotte’s suburbs that emerged in the early 1900s and as lying between NoDa and Plaza Midwood. Villa Heights was also among four neighborhoods that united under the name the Mill District. Parkwood Avenue was formerly known as County Home Road.

The account describes mill houses associated with textile workers along North Davidson Street being torn down and replaced by craftsman-style homes. The Parkwood Avenue Associate Reformed Presbyterian Church received local historic landmark designation. It opened in 1929 as Villa Heights Associate Reformed Presbyterian Church, and its building was sold to a developer in 2017 and converted into multi-unit housing. Louis Asbury, Sr. is identified as the architect. Cordelia Park was reported to have received a new playground, community center, and gated parking lot.
